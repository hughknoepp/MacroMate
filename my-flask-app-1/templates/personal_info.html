How these numbers are calculated

BMI (Body Mass Index)

Your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres, squared.

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

If you choose imperial units, your entries are first converted: 1 lb = 0.453592 kg and 1 in = 0.0254 m. The categories are: Underweight below 18.5, Normal weight 18.5–24.9, Overweight 25–29.9, and Obesity 30 or above. BMI doesn't tell muscle from fat, so treat it as a rough guide.

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)

The calories your body burns at rest, estimated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years) + 5  (male)
BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years) − 161  (female)

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure)

Your BMR multiplied by a factor for how active you are.

TDEE = BMR × activity factor

  • Sedentary: × 1.2
  • Lightly active: × 1.375
  • Moderately active: × 1.55
  • Very active: × 1.725

Calorie suggestions

All ranges are built from your TDEE and rounded to the nearest 10 calories.

  • Maintain: your TDEE ± 5%.
  • Lose: 250–750 calories below your TDEE, which works out to roughly 0.2–0.7 kg (0.5–1.5 lb) per week at about 7,700 calories per kg. It never goes below 1,500 calories/day for men or 1,200 for women, and it isn't suggested if your BMI is underweight or you are under 18.
  • Gain: 250–500 calories above your TDEE, roughly 0.2–0.5 kg (0.5–1 lb) per week. It isn't suggested if you are under 18.
