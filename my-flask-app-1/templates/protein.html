How these numbers are worked out

{% if not rec.minor %}

Each target is a number of grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day, multiplied by your weight. {% if rec.adjusted %}Because your BMI is 30 or more, the weight used is an adjusted {{ basis_text }}: the weight at a BMI of 25 plus a quarter of your extra weight, so body fat doesn't inflate the target. {% else %}The weight used is your own, {{ basis_text }}.{% endif %}

  • Baseline: the official daily allowance is 0.8 g per kg, the minimum for a healthy adult. People who are more active need more.
  • Active people: sports-nutrition guidance is roughly 1.2–2.0 g per kg.
  • Losing weight: targets are higher, because extra protein helps you keep muscle while eating less.
  • Gaining weight: up to about 2.2 g per kg supports building muscle, together with strength training.
  • Age 65 and over: at least 1.0 g per kg to maintain, and 1.2 g per kg when changing weight.

Amounts are rounded to the nearest 5 g. The share of calories uses the middle of each calorie suggestion on the personal information page, and a target is never allowed above 35% of those calories, the top of the usual 10–35% range.
